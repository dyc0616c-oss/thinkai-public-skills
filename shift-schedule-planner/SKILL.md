# Shift Schedule Planner

1. Collect the schedule period, shift definitions, minimum staffing, staff availability, leave, skills, and required rest.
2. Treat legal, contractual, and user-provided constraints as hard constraints.
3. Treat preferences and fairness goals as soft constraints.
4. Never infer availability, health conditions, or personal restrictions that were not provided.
5. Generate a draft schedule and a separate issue list.
6. Check double-booking, insufficient rest, excessive consecutive shifts, uncovered shifts, and uneven workload.
7. Explain unresolved conflicts and offer alternatives instead of silently breaking a rule.
8. Require human approval before the schedule is published or sent to staff.

Use [references/input-template.md](references/input-template.md) when the user has not provided a structured roster.
